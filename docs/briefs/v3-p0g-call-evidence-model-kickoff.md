## WHY — ruling D3, and what it broke

**D3 is decided: `tel:`-only plus a captured outcome. No telephony bridge.** Two
independent reasons, and the second is the load-bearing one.

Cost: a Twilio bridge is two billed legs, roughly TTD 2.32/min if the agent talks through
the app and TTD 4.58/min if it dials their mobile — about TTD 700-1,400 per agent per
month against a 36-call weekly minimum. It charges per unit of the exact behaviour the app
exists to increase.

**Coverage, which matters more.** Tatil agents call from personal mobiles and sometimes
company lines. A bridge only ever sees calls routed through the app, so an agent who made
36 real calls from their own phone shows **zero**. That is the source build's worst defect
in new clothing — a manager-facing screen reporting a false fact about a named agent,
generated by a coverage gap instead of a scoping bug. Partial evidence is worse than
honest declaration: a declared outcome covers every call, a bridge covers only the
obedient ones.

Microsoft Graph PSTN call records are the eventual upgrade *if* Tatil moves to Teams
Phone — `calleeNumber`, `startDateTime`, `duration` in connected seconds, `callType` for
direction, zero marginal cost, and it covers company lines natively. It is contingent on a
decision with no date and needs a tenant-wide `CallRecords.Read.All` grant, so **do not
build it.** Build so it can arrive.

### What this breaks in P0-B

P0-B also already anticipated the shape. It carries a comment that the dials-vs-contacts

it"*, with `contacts` deferred to Phase 2.1. **The disposition this slice defines is that

## 1. The record — one shape, three parts

A call is an **attempt**, optionally carrying a **disposition**, optionally carrying a
**verification**.

- Identity and time: `id`, `date`, `atHour` — unchanged, because `pcBreakdown` already
  depends on exactly these and it is property-tested.
- Attribution: the lead and the agent.
- `disposition` — the agent's own account of what happened. Vocabulary is yours to
  propose from claim 3, but it must at minimum separate *reached a person* from *did not*.
- `verification` — absent by default. A block a later source fills: connected seconds,
  direction, the source that asserted it.

**Do not invent a `contacted: true` flag.** Defect-classes section 3: every consequence is
a real object. The disposition is the record of what happened; a boolean beside it is a
twin.

## 2. The split that makes this honest — attempt vs contact

This is the core of the slice and the reason no third provenance tier is needed.

- **A dial is EVIDENCED by the record itself.** A per-call row that is timestamped and
  attached to a named lead is real evidence that an attempt was made. It is not proof of a
  connection, and it does not claim to be. PC keeps counting exactly as it does now.
- **A contact is DECLARED until something verifies it.** Whether a human answered is the
  agent's word. It belongs in the declared lane with the percentage-evidenced visible,
  like every other typed figure.

So `pcBreakdown` is **untouched** — a dial counts regardless of disposition, and the
`max(dials, itemised)` logic that took two rounds to get right is not to be re-opened.
Disposition is additive: it feeds the contacts figure and nothing else.

If you find yourself needing to change `pcBreakdown`, **STOP and report.** That is a sign
the split is wrong, not a licence.

## 3. Verification arrives late, and counts may RISE

This settles one of the three questions P0-B deliberately left open. *"Can a past day's
evidence change?"* was hypothetical. Under a Teams-style source it arrives hours after the
call, so the answer is **yes, retroactively** — and a day's evidenced contact count must be
able to go **up** after the fact.

`applyVerification(call, verification)` is pure and returns a new call. It never mutates,
never invents a disposition the agent did not give, and never silently overwrites one that
disagrees — **a verification that contradicts the agent's disposition is a finding, and the
return value must make the disagreement visible rather than resolving it.** Which of the
two wins is a product decision nobody has made; do not make it by implementation.

## 4. The properties — this is the deliverable, not the shape

`fast-check`, `numRuns: 200`, fixed seed, same budget discipline as P0-B. Report the
wall-clock added.

- **Monotonicity under logging.** Adding a call never decreases any derived count.
- **Monotonicity under verification.** Adding a verification to an existing call never
  decreases any derived count. This is the new one and it is the whole point of section 3.
- **Disposition-independence of PC.** A call's contribution to the dials figure is
  identical whatever its disposition. Generate every disposition value and assert it.
- **Contacts never exceed calls**, on any day, under any generator.
- **Never blended.** Evidenced and declared contacts are separate fields; there must be no
  function that returns their sum. If a consumer wants a total it adds two labelled numbers
  itself — enforced by the *shape* of the return value, per P0-B section 2.

**Mutation-verify at least the verification-monotonicity property**: break it deliberately,
paste the shrunk counterexample, restore, paste it passing. A property that has never
failed may not be wired to anything.
