Financing Self-View — the subject's own picture, framed as "how close am I to free?"

A new, read-only, subject-facing surface — not one of the six manager sheets. One component, two audiences: an Agent, and a financed Unit Manager viewing their own financing under My Production. Same layout for both — the field set is owner-locked; the job here is hierarchy: which shown fields earn the motivational hero slot vs. which are utilitarian statement readouts. Currency TTD; dates DD-MM-YYYY.

Data canon

Read-only projection of the subject's own financing. Reads financingTerms/{subjectId} + the monthly financing/{subjectId}_{YYYY_MM} ledger + the reconciliation record + statusHistory. No writes, no manager controls. The confirmed monthly draw is relabelled to "Financing this month / Your draw" — never "Manager Financing." All thresholds (6× ceiling, 25% tax, gate values) are configurable ruleset placeholders. Both light + dark via Nexus tokens; hero uses the Nexus Glass tier (one per screen).

◆ Option A · recommended

Hero = the paydown arc + wind-down clocks

A self-view's standing question is "when does this end?" — so the always-on, forward-looking hero is the running-balance arc trending to zero, paired with the two clocks (months to waiver, months to term). Achievement-framed, relevant every month — not just at payout.

◇ Option B · the alternative

Hero = "what you keep" take-home

Lead with the most emotional number — what actually lands in your pocket this month after the draw comes out. Immediate and visceral, and it's the same expectation-management story as the K4 waterfall.

HeroWhat you keep · take-home waterfall

⚖ Tradeoff

I recommend A. "What you keep" is the stronger single number, but it's backward-looking and bonus-cyclical — in a month with no bonus it's just net-minus-draw, and the take-home waterfall already exists as the K4 component shown at payout. The paydown arc is always-on and forward-looking: it gives a financed person the one thing a statement never does — a sense of trajectory and an end date. B keeps take-home as a strong secondary hero, so its emotional payload isn't lost. Pick one before port — mobile shows only ~1.3 cards above the fold, so the hero choice is load-bearing.

Why this order survives the fold

On a phone the subject sees roughly one-and-a-bit cards before scrolling. Option A spends that budget on the question they actually carry: balance, trajectory, end date.

  • 1
    Hero stays whole — status badge, the arc, and both clocks fit one glass card without scrolling. The arc compresses but keeps the now-dot + projected-clear tail.
  • 2
    "What you keep" rides second — one big number, no waterfall on mobile (that expands on tap, reusing K4). It's the emotional hook without crowding the hero.
  • 3
    Utilitarian cards collapse — statement, terms, reconciliation and timeline stack below, each a tight fact list. 44px min touch targets throughout; nothing here is tappable-to-edit (read-only).

Identical — the entire component
Same hero, same hierarchy (Option A), same cards, same field set, same read-only rule, same basis badge, same privacy allow-list. The Unit Manager self-view is not a separate screen — it's the K9 component bound to financingTerms/{theirId}.
Δ
Differs — only identity & context
Three things: (1) reached via My Production → My Financing, not the agent's primary nav; (2) the subject is the UM's own record (their name, term, balance); (3) the breadcrumb sits alongside their My Unit tools. Nothing structural changes.
✕
Forbidden — no manager bleed-through
Because this person is a manager, the risk is leakage. None of their manager affordances appear here: no override, no suggested-draw, no roster, and crucially their own adjustmentPct stays invisible — the 5.3 trigger is their manager's internal, not theirs to see.
Annotations
1
Hero — paydown arc, not a statement number
The glass hero (one per screen ✓) renders runningBalance as an arc to zero: solid line = actual to month 7, dashed = projected to clear. A negative balance reads as surplus (crosses below the zero baseline), never an error. This is the one thing a statement can't give — trajectory.
2
Wind-down clocks live in the hero
serviceMonths/serviceMet → "months to 12-mo waiver"; term length → "months to term." Two countdowns that frame the whole view as "how close to free." Met state turns green; serviceMet:true flips the waiver clock to "✓ waiver earned."
3
"Financing this month / Your draw" — the relabel
LOCKED: the confirmed monthly draw never reads "Manager Financing" on a self-view. It renders as "Financing this month" with a "your draw" tag. Same number, same basisSource badge — just the subject's-eye label.
4
"What you keep" reuses the K4 waterfall
Secondary hero. Desktop shows netCommission − financingPaid − bonusOffset as a 3-line keep-list; tapping it on mobile expands the full gross→tax→net→−50%→take-home waterfall (K4 component). No new calc — same bonusOffset that posts to the ledger.
5
Basis badge, unchanged
Every figure carries its basisSource: month 7 reads Submitted — provisional (trues up to settled). The same three-state badge from every Track-K surface — no new primitive, no new color token.
6
Status history is transitions-only
Renders statusHistory as a {from → to, date} timeline. The byName / role / note inside each entry are private and stripped — a subject sees that their status changed and when, never who moved it or their note.
7
FLAG — does the projected reconciliation show pre-emptively?
The year-end card shows a waiver-adjusted projected closing position while still on_financing. Confirm a subject should see waiverApplied / surplus projected (motivational) vs. only at the actual event. Drawn pre-emptive, labelled PROJECTED — your call.
8
FLAG — Agent vs Unit-Mgr-own: identical, confirm
One component, two audiences. The role switch in the topbar is illustrative only — the layout, fields, and privacy rules are identical for both. Confirm a Unit Manager viewing their own financing under My Production gets exactly the agent self-view, no manager affordances bleed in.
9
Bonus qualification lives here — but compact
A self-view earns a bonus-gate module because qualifying is the lever that clears the financing: a qualifying quarter posts the ~50% bonusOffset and pulls the paydown arc's clear-date earlier. It leads with validated API vs the $37,500 gate. This is the glanceable version — the full quarterly tracker stays on the K8 Validation Dashboard; this links to it rather than duplicating it. Same module for agent + financed unit manager.
The privacy boundary — what this surface must NOT render
🔒

Owner-ruled private. Manager internals never reach a self-view.

These exist on the record and drive manager surfaces (K1–K8), but are excluded by rule here. The self-view reads the same documents with a field allow-list — it does not fetch-then-hide. Critically, the 5.3 termination-trigger ratio is invisible to the subject.

⚑ Flags

Two decisions gate the port. (1) Hero choice — Option A (paydown arc) vs B (take-home); I recommend A, B annotated. (2) Pre-emptive projection — whether the year-end surplus/waiver shows while still on_financing (drawn yes, labelled PROJECTED). Every threshold (6× · 25% · gate values) is a ruleset placeholder, not a constant. The privacy allow-list is not negotiable — adjustmentPct and all audit/byName fields must be unreachable from this surface, not merely hidden.
