Nexus Glass · HERO variant

Teal made of glass — the bold hero card, reborn.

A deep tinted glass hero tier: the old solid-teal card presence (saturated, bold, brand-forward) made translucent — the backdrop still visibly blurs through it. Separate light/dark physics, a fully inverted (light-on-saturated) ink set, and AA floors computed against the composite, not the raw surface. The subtle white-frosted recipe stays as the non-hero tier for secondary cards.

The shift

"Teal made of glass," not "glass with a teal tint." The pane is a saturated teal/gold gradient at 0.86–0.96 alpha — deep enough to invert the ink world to white, translucent enough that the blur reads. This inverts everything: text goes light, status becomes chip islands (each its own controlled tile), and the contrast floor is computed at the pane's lightest gradient stop over the lightest app surface — the AA worst case.

P
Translucent, not opaque — the blur must read
Panes sit at 0.86–0.96 alpha (dark theme runs more translucent since the backdrop is already dark). High enough to invert inks to white & hold a deterministic floor; low enough that backdrop-filter: blur visibly bends the backdrop through the glass. That's the "liquid" in liquid-glass.
G
Gold hero = white-on-deep-gold (resolved)
A light gold pane can't read as a bold hero and carry dark text that pops. Resolved by going deep amber (#8A6010→#6E4C0C) so the ink world stays inverted to white — consistent with the teal hero. Dark-on-light-gold is rejected for the hero tier.

S
Status renders as chip islands — like the old solid-teal cards' white chips
A saturated pane can't carry dark status ink (it fails AA), so status becomes a tinted island: a white-@15% inset tile (its own mini-glass) holding a light label + a status DOT. The hue lives in the dot (a graphical element certified at 3:1 against the island, which is lighter than the pane — so the dot has room), never in dark text on the pane. Value + label use --hero-ink / --hero-ink-muted.
AA floors · computed against the COMPOSITE (not the raw surface)
Floor rule

glassPair() composites pane(lightest stop) → over → darkest named surface to an opaque effective color, then certifies every hero ink against it. The prior subtle recipe computed against the raw surface — this corrects that: the hero floor is the pane's lightest gradient stop flattened over the lightest light-theme surface (worst case for white text). Values below are design-time targets; the module is the authority. If a saturation can't carry the muted tier, lower the saturation — never the 4.5 threshold.

Test hook

Extend glassPair() with hero variants. glassPair('hero-teal','light') → composite rgba(1,105,111,.93) over surfaceMute #F0EFE9 → #167275; assert {hero-ink, hero-ink-muted, hero-accent} ≥ 4.5 and each hero-dot-* ≥ 3:1 against hero-chip-island composited on the same floor. Repeat for hero-gold and both themes. The muted tiers sit closest to the line (4.6–4.8) — they are the canaries: if a future tint shift drops them under 4.5, lower that pane's lightest-stop alpha or deepen its hue.

📽 Meeting Mode
REDUCED — blur ≤ 8px

Half blur, saturation 110%; keep the deep pane + lit rim + chip islands. Reads identical at projector distance without the per-frame filter cost.

📱 In-app + reduced-transparency
FULL HERO GLASS

One hero per screen, full recipe. prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce flattens to the same deep solids (rim + shadow kept) — the inverted ink set is unchanged, so nothing else shifts.

Tiers

Two tiers now coexist. Hero (this doc) = the deep tinted glass the 3 flagship cards adopt. Subtle (the prior white-frosted recipe) is retained as the non-hero tier for secondary cards that may want a lighter glass later — it keeps the dark-ink world and its own floors. A card is one or the other, never both; the hero tier stays one card per screen (the census's "gets glass" list).
