17: LOOKING AHEAD
18: "A consistent finding in motivation research is that challenging, realistic goals lead to better performance than do no goals, easy goals, or extremely difficult goals. It's a fact that producers who set and work toward production goals outperform other producers; they have a clear understanding of what their goals are and how to achieve them. In fact, goal-setting is one of the strongest predictors of production."
19: -from LIMRA's The Productivity Planner
20: Follow this six-step plan to organize your production for this year and to start looking ahead to next year and beyond.
21: Step 1 -Organize your money needs for the year.
22: Determine your money needs for next year using the budget form shown on page 3. By determining how much income you must have during the year, you can calculate how much you must earn in first-year life commissions. Note: Additional sections are provided throughout this book for recording activity and results for other lines, as well, such as annuities, disability income, automobile, homeowners, etc.
23: Step 2 -Organize your activities for the year.
24: To earn the commissions needed to meet your money needs you must have prospects, secure appointments, make presentations, ask prospects to buy, secure applications, deliver policies, and collect first-year premiums from which your first-year commissions will be derived. From Step 1, you know how much you must earn. In Step 2, you translate this income into activities -your minimum requirements for gathering new qualified prospects, making personal (seen) calls, conducting interviews, etc. -in terms of annual, monthly, and weekly quotas.
25: Step 3 -Organize your plans for self-improvement.
26: To reach your objectives, you must work effectively. Are you working as effectively as you did last year? To answer this question, rate your performance using the chart on page 6. Each of five major areas that determine your effectiveness as a producer is broken down into specific functions that directly affect your performance. Your ratings will help you to pinpoint those areas in which you are not as effective as you would like to be and to develop plans for improving your effectiveness. Rate yourself quarterly to evaluate how your self-improvement plans are working.
27: Step 4 -Organize your prospecting plans.
28: In a normal day, a continuous stream of prospect cards flows into and out of your prospect file. At least once a month all cards should be removed from your file preparatory to planning your prospecting and selling activities for the next 30 days; enter these prospects' names on the appropriate page of your Monthly Prospecting Work Sheet (pages 8-31). Some suggestions for organizing your prospecting plans appear on page 7.
29: Step 5 -Organize your record keeping.
30: The best way to stay on track with your sales and self-improvement activities is to keep good records. Use the charts on pages 44, 45, and 58-61 to track your progress toward your objectives. These figures come from your monthly activity summaries on pages 32--43 and from your record of written and paid business on pages 46-57. Information recorded on these pages (source of prospect, interview on which sale closed, cash with application, etc.) also gives you valuable feedback about your markets and sales methods that will help you monitor your progress toward self-improvement.
31: On pages 58-61, post your monthly commissions as a further check on your financial progress. Use pages
32: 62-63 to record your business expenses and to determine the profit from your activities.
33: Remember that the real value of record keeping is that it provides valuable feedback on your performance that will keep you on track toward accomplishing your objectives. With proper record keeping you will always know where you stand and should never fall so far behind in your objectives that you can't catch up.
34: Step 6 -Organize your long-range plans.
35: An important part of planning for the future is predicting what that future will be. Start by using the Future Prospects form on pages 64-65 to list the prospects and policy owners you plan to see next year. Then use the Long-Range Planning form on page 66 to prepare a three-year plan for yourself. Make sure that your objectives are based on past results and your best estimates of what is possible. You can then meet or surpass these objectives by following through on your plans with specific actions to improve performance.
36: Looking ahead to next year and beyond can help you achieve your most ambitious goals, if you carry out the plans that you have made.
