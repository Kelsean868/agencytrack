Persistency v2 — moving to a rolling, per-policy, time-weighted model

Tatil is proposing a change to how persistency is calculated. This is the analysis: what changes versus how we operate today, what it does to the Persistency surface and the what-if Playground, and what we need to build to ship it behind a tenant-admin toggle we can flip when Tatil confirms — rumoured as early as July.

Source: Supporting Notes — Persistency (Tatil) Audience: Product + Engineering Switch target: July (unconfirmed) Rollout: Tenant-wide flag

01 The short version

A timing-blind portfolio ratio becomes a timing-aware, self-expiring per-policy score.
  • Today (v1): persistency is one aggregate ratio — Net Settled ÷ Gross Settled — over a fixed 12- or 24-month reporting window. A lapse counts against the agent for the whole window no matter when it happened.
  • Proposed (v2): every policy is tracked individually across a rolling 24-month lifecycle. A lapse is a debit weighted by the months remaining to 24; a reinstatement is a credit for the remaining months. Impact declines over time and self-expires at 24 months. Persistency becomes Credits − Debits, time-weighted, traceable per policy and agent.
  • Net effect: early lapses hurt more, late lapses hurt less, reinstating quickly recovers most of the penalty — and the number on screen changes for every agent the moment the model flips.
  • How we ship it: a single tenant-wide persistency_model setting (v1 → v2) in Settings › Policy & Thresholds, company-locked, default v1. One calc engine serves the reality bar, roster, playground, and awards so they never disagree.

02 What does not change

The input definitions carry over. Only the aggregation and weighting change.

The underlying business definitions in the notes are unchanged — keep these intact in both engines:

Gross Settled Business = business settled in the period − Not Takens + Increase PPPs + 10% Lumpsums Net Settled Business = Gross Settled − lapses + reinstatements

What changes is the basis (portfolio → per-policy), the window (fixed report cycle → rolling 24 months from each policy's start), and the introduction of time-weighting.

03 How we calculate persistency today (v1)

One ratio, one window, timing-blind.
Persistency = Net Settled ÷ Gross Settled × 100

As built in the repo: each agent's monthly persistency is derived from six manager-entered figures (gross settled, net settled, lapses, reinstatements, policies in force, policies issued), banded against an 80% floor and a 90% award gate, then averaged to a branch aggregate. Manager entry overrides agent self-entry and locks the month.

The properties that the notes call out as the problem:

  • A lapse remains on the report for the full 12 or 24 months regardless of when it happened — a policy that lasted 6 months penalises the agent for the entire window.
  • No distinction between an early lapse and a late one.
  • Reinstatement only adjusts aggregate totals within the reporting window; impact ends arbitrarily with the report period.
  • It is a lagging, portfolio-level indicator that can over- or under-penalise and is disconnected from the actual policy lifecycle.

04 The proposed model (v2)

Net Impact = Credits − Debits, weighted by months remaining to 24.

Every policy is followed for 24 months from its start. A lapse books a debit; a reinstatement books a credit; both are scaled by how much of the 24-month lifecycle is left when the event occurs, so impact declines over time and clears itself at 24 months (or after 24 premiums).

The mechanics, concretely

Let rem(m) = max(0, 24 − m) be the months remaining in the lifecycle at month m. For each policy i with annualised premium APIi:

debitMonths = rem(lapseMonth) if lapsed, else 0 creditMonths = rem(reinstateMonth) if reinstated, else 0 netPenaltyMonths = max(0, debitMonths − creditMonths) weightedDebit = API × netPenaltyMonths ÷ 24 Persistency v2 = (Σ API − Σ weightedDebit) ÷ Σ API × 100

A neat identity falls out: a lapse-then-reinstate nets to reinstateMonth − lapseMonth penalty-months — the agent is charged only for the months the policy was actually lapsed.

Worked examples (matching the notes)

ScenarioDebitCreditNet penaltyPenalty weight
Lapse after 6 months (no reinstatement)18 mo—18 mo75%

Same lapse, very different cost: month-6 lapse = 75% weight, month-18 lapse = 25%. Reinstating the month-6 lapse at month 10 collapses the penalty to ~17% — the notes' "4 months penalty equivalent."

06 Impact on the Persistency Playground

This is the surface that changes most — the calc, the levers, and the coaching priority all shift.

Today the Playground treats the agent's book as one aggregate ratio. Its levers — "policies saved / month" and "additional clean API / quarter" — push a flat percentage-point bump and project a single new number against the 90% gate. Under v2 the projection has to be computed per policy and per timing, which changes the advice it gives.

Same book, two numbers

Take Priya Naidu's book — TTD 13,000 gross, four policies currently lapsed. Under v1 her three unreinstated lapses (TTD 3,640) knock her to 72% — below the floor. Under v2 the same book re-weights to ~85.8%, because her largest lapse happened late in its lifecycle and barely dents the score:

The flip from 72% (below floor) to 85.8% (watch band) comes purely from re-weighting — no policy changed. K. Baksh's late lapse drops from a full −1,960 hit to −490.

The coaching priority flips

This is the most important behavioural change for the Playground:

  • Under v1, the highest-leverage move is reinstating the biggest-API lapse (K. Baksh, TTD 1,960) — it adds the full amount back to Net, worth roughly +15 pp.
  • Under v2, the highest-leverage move is reinstating the earliest lapse (A. Gopaul, lapsed month 6) — its debit is large and the credit recovered is large, so reinstating now is worth ~+6 pp, while reinstating K. Baksh is worth under +2 pp. And the value decays every month as rem() falls.

So the Playground can no longer rank at-risk policies by API alone — it must rank by recoverable time-weighted credit, and it must surface the decay ("reinstating A. Gopaul this month is worth +6 pp; next month, +5 pp").

What the Playground UI needs

  • Each at-risk policy shows months into lifecycle, its current time-weighted penalty, and the credit recoverable if reinstated now.
  • Levers become per-policy actions (reinstate / save before lapse) rather than abstract monthly counts — each with its own model-aware pp impact.
  • Policies past 24 months drop off automatically ("self-expired") and stop counting.
  • A provenance chip — "Model: v2 · rolling 24-mo" — so the agent knows which basis the projection uses.

Other surfaces touched

  • Reality bar & roster: the aggregate % and per-agent bands recompute from the new engine; "below floor / award-eligible" counts will move.
  • Manager entry: the six monthly figures are not sufficient for v2 — it needs per-policy lapse and reinstatement dates. Entry shifts from typing totals to confirming a policy ledger.
  • Awards / commission: anything gated on the 90% threshold (Eagles Club, MDRT eligibility) inherits the re-weighted numbers — confirm the gate stays at 90%.
  • Exception alerts: "persistency below floor" triggers fire on the new basis.

07 What we need to implement it

A per-policy data grain, one switchable calc engine, and a tenant flag.

7.1 — Data model (the biggest lift)

v2 needs policy-grain records with dates, not monthly aggregates:

policy { id, agentId, startDate, status, lapseDate?, reinstateDate?, api, premiumCount }

We already render a Policy Ledger surface, so the lapse/reinstate events likely exist — confirm it carries reliable lapseDate and reinstateDate per policy. If those dates are missing for historical policies, v2 cannot weight them (see restatement, below).

7.2 — Calc engine

Centralise into one pure function so every surface agrees:

persistency(book, model, asOf) → percent // model ∈ { 'v1', 'v2' }

v1 and v2 sit behind one interface; the reality bar, roster, Playground, manager entry and awards all call it. No surface should compute persistency inline.

7.3 — The tenant admin toggle

  • Add a persistency_model tenant setting: enum v1 | v2, default v1, company-locked — only the tenant admin can flip it.
  • It belongs in Settings › Policy & Thresholds, directly beneath the existing "Persistency threshold (80%)" row, rendered as the standard locked segmented control.
  • Flipping it switches the engine for the entire tenant in one move (per your call — tenant-wide, not staged).
  • Gate the flip behind a confirm dialog — "This restates persistency for the whole organisation" — and capture an effective date for the audit log.
  • Every persistency surface renders a small provenance chip showing the active model so no one misreads a v2 number as v1.

7.4 — Restatement / cutover

Built as a second tenant toggleBecause Tatil hasn't confirmed whether v2 applies going forward only or restates history, we expose it as its own company-locked setting — Persistency restatement scope — beside the model switch, so we're ready either way:
  • Going forward — prior periods keep their original v1 basis; v2 applies from a defined "v2 epoch" date. Safe default; needs no historical lifecycle data.
  • Restate history — all prior periods are recomputed under the active model. Cleaner comparisons, but requires reliable per-policy lapse/reinstate dates for older policies (see §7.1) — flag a fallback where they're missing.

7.5 — Rollout

  1. Ship both engines; flag defaults to v1 — zero visible change.
  2. Enable v2 in staging against a sample book; validate the engine reproduces Tatil's worked examples exactly.
  3. On Tatil's written confirmation, the tenant admin flips the flag in production.
  4. Keep the v1 engine in code for at least two reporting cycles for audit and one-click rollback.

7.6 — Risks

  • Numbers move for everyone at once (72% → 85.8% in our example). Needs comms; award eligibility shifts the same day.
  • Data gaps: a policy missing a lapse/reinstate date can't be weighted — define a fallback (e.g. treat as rem() at report start) and flag it in the UI.
  • Definition ambiguity (§04 flag) — building before sign-off risks a re-do.
